CS2, Actually Explained

Enough to stop quietly throwing rounds. The game isn't really about aim — it's about buying together and using your nades. Five minutes, then go win.

1

What you're actually doing

Five of you vs five of them. Two sides, swapping at halftime. One side plants a bomb, the other stops it.

Terrorists (T)

Your job: get the bomb to one of two bomb sites (A or B), plant it, and keep it alive for ~40 seconds until it blows. Or just kill all five CTs.

Counter-Terrorists (CT)

Your job: stop the plant, or defuse the bomb if it's down. Or kill all five Ts. Hold the sites; don't let them in cheap.

How you win a round

  • Ts win by detonating the bomb, or eliminating all CTs.
  • CTs win by defusing the bomb, eliminating all Ts, or running out the clock with no bomb planted.

How you win the match

Format is MR12: 12 rounds per half, first team to 13 rounds wins. You play one side for the first half, then swap sides and play the other. If it ends 12–12, you go to overtime (a short MR3 mini-match, first to 4, with money reset each time).

2

Anatomy of a round

Every round is the same loop: a short buy/freeze window, the live round, an end state, then reset and go again.

During freeze time you can't move out, but you can buy. Spend it talking: what are we buying, who drops who, where are we going.

4

Buying as a team

The single biggest fix for casual games: buy together or save together. Five half-buys lose to five full-buys every time.

Full buy

Everyone has a primary gun, armour, and a couple of nades. The standard "we're going for it" round.

Force buy

You don't quite have enough, but you spend everything anyway because you can't afford to lose this round (e.g. enemy match point).

Eco

Buy almost nothing on purpose so next round everyone can full-buy. Maybe a pistol upgrade and a nade.

Save

You're already armed but can't win — keep your guns alive for next round instead of feeding them.

💡 Drop for your broke mate. If you can afford two guns, buy one and drop it for the teammate who can't. Five guns > four guns + your bank balance.
5

Roles, casually

Not job titles — just "who does what" so you're not all doing the same thing.

The opener (entry)

Goes in first to make space and take the first fight. Brave, dies a lot, that's the job.

The trade

Right behind the entry. When the entry dies, you instantly kill whoever killed them — so it's a 1-for-1, not a loss.

The anchor / lurk

Holds a quiet area or a site alone, watches flanks, catches rotators sneaking across.

The AWPer

Carries the big sniper. Holds long angles, picks people off. Pricey, so don't lose it cheap.

The caller (IGL)

Decides the buy and the plan each round. Doesn't have to be the best aim — just the clearest head.

…and everyone

Throws utility, trades teammates, gives short clear info. Roles are loose — cover what's missing.

6

Utility 101

Grenades. At our level these win more rounds than aim does — a good smoke beats a good crosshair.

🧠 The point: you don't have to out-aim anyone if they can't see you, can't push through fire, and got blinded walking in. Buy nades. Throw them.
7

5 habits that win casual games

Do these and you'll climb without getting any better at shooting.

1
Buy together. Full-buy as five, or save as five. Never one rifle alone into a stacked site.
2
Trade your teammate. When a mate dies in front of you, re-peek immediately and kill the guy who got them. One-for-one is fine; dying alone isn't.
3
Actually use your nades. Smoke the angle, flash for your entry, molly the corner. Dead-money grenades are wasted rounds.
4
Don't peek one at a time into the same angle. Feeding an AWPer five solo duels is five free kills. Push together or smoke it off.
5
Play for the bomb. Plant it (Ts), defuse/deny it (CTs). The objective wins rounds and pays better than chasing frags.
8

Quick glossary

Eco
A round where you deliberately buy little to save money for the next one.
Force buy
Spending everything you have now because you can't afford to lose this round.
Full buy
Gun + armour + nades for everyone — the standard committed round.
Save
Keeping your weapons alive in a lost round instead of dying with them.
Trade
Killing the enemy who just killed your teammate, so it's an even exchange.
Lurk
Playing away from your team to catch rotations or hit from behind.
Rotate
Moving from one site to the other because that's where the action is.
Retake
Taking back a bomb site (usually as CT) after the enemy already has it.
Default
A calm standard opening: spread out, grab map control, decide late.
Anti-eco
A round where you know the enemy is poor — play safe, don't hand them free guns.
Clutch
Winning a round alone when your whole team is dead. Hero stuff.
